Rules draft

Important note

Casino Craps has many betting options and can look intimidating. For casual play, start with the basic Pass Line version. That teaches the heart of the game without needing a casino layout.

Object in the simple version

Players bet on whether the shooter will win or lose according to the Pass Line rules.

The shooter is the person rolling the dice.

The come-out roll

The first roll of a round is called the come-out roll.

Roll two dice and add them together.

On the come-out roll:

  • If the shooter rolls 7 or 11, the shooter wins immediately.
  • If the shooter rolls 2, 3, or 12, the shooter loses immediately. This is called craps.
  • If the shooter rolls 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, or 10, that number becomes the point.

If a point is established

Once there is a point, the shooter keeps rolling until one of two things happens:

  • The shooter rolls the point again and wins.
  • The shooter rolls a 7 and loses. This is called sevening out.

Other numbers do not end the round. They are ignored for the basic version.

Example round

  1. Shooter rolls 5 on the come-out roll.
  2. The point is now 5.
  3. Shooter keeps rolling.
  4. If the shooter rolls 5 again before rolling 7, the shooter wins.
  5. If the shooter rolls 7 before rolling 5, the shooter loses.

Simple home scoring without money

Instead of betting money, use tokens, chips, pennies, or points.

Option A: Everyone starts with 10 tokens.

  • If the shooter wins, anyone betting with the shooter gains 1 token.
  • If the shooter loses, anyone betting with the shooter loses 1 token.

Option B: Rotate shooters and track wins.

  • Shooter gets 1 point for a win.
  • Next person becomes shooter after a loss, or after a set number of rounds.

Why it works

Craps has a strong rhythm: immediate wins/losses on the first roll, then suspense once a point is set. The full casino version is complicated, but the core game is actually simple.
