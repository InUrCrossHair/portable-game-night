Important note

Casino Craps has many betting options and can look intimidating. For casual play, use this basic Pass Line version. It teaches the heart of the game without needing a casino layout.

Object in the simple version

Players bet tokens or points on whether the shooter will win the round.

The shooter is the person rolling the dice.

Setup

Choose one of two scoring styles:

  • Token style: each player starts with 10 tokens.
  • Points style: rotate shooters and track shooter wins.

The come-out roll

The first roll of a round is called the come-out roll.

Roll two dice and add them together.

On the come-out roll:

  • If the shooter rolls 7 or 11, the shooter wins immediately.
  • If the shooter rolls 2, 3, or 12, the shooter loses immediately. This is called craps.
  • If the shooter rolls 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, or 10, that number becomes the point.

If a point is established

Once there is a point, the shooter keeps rolling until one of two things happens:

  • The shooter rolls the point again and wins.
  • The shooter rolls a 7 and loses. This is called sevening out.

Other numbers do not end the round. They are ignored in this simple version.

Token style

Everyone starts with 10 tokens.

Before the come-out roll, players choose whether they are betting with the shooter or against the shooter.

  • If the shooter wins, players betting with the shooter gain 1 token and players betting against lose 1 token.
  • If the shooter loses, players betting against the shooter gain 1 token and players betting with lose 1 token.

Points style

If you do not want betting at all:

  • Rotate shooters around the table.
  • The shooter gets 1 point for a win.
  • First to 5 shooter wins, or most wins after each player has been shooter 3 times, wins the game.

Why it works

Craps has a strong rhythm: immediate wins/losses on the first roll, then suspense once a point is set. The full casino version is complicated, but the core game is actually simple.
