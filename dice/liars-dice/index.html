Rules draft

Object

Be the last player with dice remaining.

Setup

  1. Give each player 5 dice and a cup.
  2. Everyone rolls their dice secretly under the cup.
  3. Players may look at their own dice, but not anyone else’s.

How bidding works

A bid has two parts:

  • A quantity
  • A die face

Example: “four 3s” means the bidder claims there are at least four dice showing 3 among all players’ hidden dice combined.

The next player must either:

  1. Raise the bid, or
  2. Call liar.

To raise, the new bid must be higher. Commonly, you can raise either the quantity or the face value depending on the rules being used. For beginners, the simplest version is:

  • Increase the quantity, such as from “four 3s” to “five 3s,” or
  • Keep the same quantity and increase the face, such as “four 3s” to “four 4s.”

Calling liar

When someone calls liar, everyone reveals their dice.

  • If the table has at least as many of that face as the bid claimed, the bidder was correct, and the caller loses one die.
  • If the table has fewer than the bid claimed, the bidder was bluffing or wrong, and the bidder loses one die.

Then everyone rerolls and a new round begins.

Common wild rule

Many versions make 1s wild, meaning a 1 can count as any number. For your first game, you may want to play without wilds until everyone understands the flow.

Why it works

Liar’s Dice is less about math and more about reading people. You know your own dice, but you are guessing what everyone else might have and whether the current bid sounds believable.
