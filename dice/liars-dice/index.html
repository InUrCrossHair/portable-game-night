Rules draft

Variant status: Ready. Official rules use wild 1s and include both elimination and drinking-game variants.

Object

Choose one of two variants before playing:

  • Elimination variant: be the last player with dice remaining.
  • Drinking-game variant: play a set number of rounds; losing a challenge means taking a drink instead of losing a die.

Setup

  1. Give each player 5 dice and a cup.
  2. Everyone rolls their dice secretly under the cup.
  3. Players may look at their own dice, but not anyone else’s.
  4. Choose the elimination variant or drinking-game variant before the first round.

Wild 1s rule

In this house version, 1s are wild. A 1 can count as any face from 2 through 6 when resolving a bid.

Example: if the bid is “four 5s,” then all 5s and all 1s count toward the total.

How bidding works

A bid has two parts:

  • A quantity
  • A die face

Example: “four 3s” means the bidder claims there are at least four dice showing 3 among all players’ hidden dice combined, counting wild 1s.

The next player must either:

  1. Raise the bid, or
  2. Call liar.

To raise, the new bid must either:

  • Increase the quantity, such as from “four 3s” to “five 3s,” or
  • Keep the same quantity and increase the face, such as “four 3s” to “four 4s.”

For simplicity, do not bid directly on 1s. They are wild support dice, not their own bid face.

Calling liar

When someone calls liar, everyone reveals their dice.

  • If the table has at least as many of that face as the bid claimed, counting wild 1s, the bidder was correct.
  • If the table has fewer than the bid claimed, the bidder was bluffing or wrong.

Elimination variant

Use this as the default family/friends version.

  • If the bidder was correct, the caller loses 1 die.
  • If the bidder was wrong, the bidder loses 1 die.
  • Everyone rerolls and a new round begins.
  • The last player with dice remaining wins.

Drinking-game variant

Use this only with adults who actually want a drinking-game version.

  • Nobody loses dice.
  • If the bidder was correct, the caller takes 1 drink.
  • If the bidder was wrong, the bidder takes 1 drink.
  • Everyone keeps all 5 dice and rerolls for the next round.
  • Play for a set number of rounds, such as 10 rounds, or until the group decides to stop.

Safety note: make drinks small, let players skip or use water, and do not pressure anyone to drink.

Why it works

Liar’s Dice is less about math and more about reading people. Wild 1s make bids more dramatic and uncertain, while the two variants let the same rules work as either a normal elimination game or a casual adult party game.
